Station Stowage Manifest

The station runs on a 5-candela flashlight, one shadow map and exponential fog. Every art decision follows from that: a player identifies an object by its outline in a torch beam, never by its surface.

Emissive means you can touch it. Every interactable object carries one small self-lit accent so it is findable with the torch off. Everything inert stays dark — the moment a non-interactive prop glows, the signal is worthless. This is the one rule that cannot bend.

Rev B — players walk now. Gravity is the default and zero-G is a per-module condition (GravityMode = 'nominal' | 'zero'). That changes what art has to do: interiors need floors, corners and cover, hiding becomes a real mechanic that needs real geometry, and a player must be able to tell a module's gravity mode before stepping through the hatch.

Gravity-era geometry

ISS-GRV · 11 assets · 0 built

New for Rev B, and the reason the pivot is worth doing. A 5 m tube gives a fleeing player nothing to use — no corners, no sightline breaks, no cover — so the alien at SPEED_HUNT 3.0 always runs down a sprinter at 2.4. These assets are the escape geometry, and every dimension is derived from a real constant rather than eyeballed. Hiding is a brand-new mechanic: the alien is blind, so hiding means it won't physically sweep through you and you must stay quiet. No sight-based logic anywhere.

Rules that decide the ambiguous cases

  • When in doubt, don't glow. The amber accent is a promise that a player can act on something. Every false positive costs you every true one.
  • Two assets that matter never share a profile. If a medkit and a decoy read the same in a torch beam at 4 m, one of them is wrong — and the player throws the wrong thing.
  • Detail goes into the outline, not the surface. No one will ever see a bevel. They will absolutely see a handle, a horn, a claw, a spoke.
  • Colour is never the only cue. Cargo bags are numbered by band count, not hue. Hatch states differ in geometry as well as indicator colour.
  • Nothing out-contrasts a handrail — inside a zero-G module. There it is the only way to move. In gravity modules that priority passes to cover and thresholds.
  • Gravity mode must be readable before you commit. A player standing at a hatch has to know whether the far side has a floor. Surprise transitions turn the pivot's best moment into a bug report.
  • Cover heights come from constants, not taste. 1.00 m hides a crouch because crouched eyes sit at 0.85 m; 0.40 m is steppable because STEP_HEIGHT_M says so. Every barrier in the station is one of those two numbers, and anything in between is a trap that reads as cover and isn't.
  • Check every asset against the body, not the room. 1.55 m standing eye, 0.85 m crouched, 0.35 m radius, 0.75 m stride. Doll furniture is the most common failure.
